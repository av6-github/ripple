# ADR-001: Polish Companies Bankruptcy Dataset Domain Modeling & Forecasting Horizon Representation

## Status
Accepted

## Date
2026-09-22

## Context
RIPPLE requires an empirical benchmark dataset for SME credit-risk and bankruptcy risk assessment. The selected dataset is the Polish Companies Bankruptcy dataset (Tomczak & Zięba, 2016; UCI Machine Learning Repository Dataset 365), consisting of five distinct ARFF files:
- `1year.arff`: 7,027 instances, 271 bankrupt (3.86%), financial ratios from 1st year of forecasting period predicting bankruptcy after 5 years.
- `2year.arff`: 10,173 instances, 400 bankrupt (3.93%), predicting bankruptcy after 4 years.
- `3year.arff`: 10,503 instances, 495 bankrupt (4.71%), predicting bankruptcy after 3 years.
- `4year.arff`: 9,792 instances, 515 bankrupt (5.26%), predicting bankruptcy after 2 years.
- `5year.arff`: 5,910 instances, 410 bankrupt (6.94%), predicting bankruptcy after 1 year.

The dataset contains 64 financial ratios ($X_1$ through $X_{64}$) derived from balance sheets and income statements, plus a binary bankruptcy target indicator (`class {0, 1}`).

## Decision

1. **Independent Forecasting Case Modeling (No Blind Concatenation):**
   The five ARFF files MUST NOT be naively merged into a single training table. Each file corresponds to an observation window with a specific forward time-to-event horizon (1 to 5 years). Naive concatenation would treat multi-year observations of the same underlying companies as independent instances, resulting in severe temporal leakage, artificial sample inflation, and corrupted validation splits.

2. **Target Semantics:**
   The dataset target `class = 1` represents legal court bankruptcy/insolvency, which is NOT identical to credit default (e.g. 90+ days past due or restructuring). RIPPLE records the outcome as `target_bankruptcy` at a specified horizon (`target_horizon_months`), clearly distinguished in reports and explanations from a pure contractual loan delinquency.

3. **Hybrid Relational + JSONB Persistence Strategy:**
   - The PostgreSQL `financial_record` table stores standard financial statement aggregates (`revenue`, `ebit`, `net_profit`, `total_assets`, `current_assets`, `total_liabilities`, `equity`, `working_capital`) to support direct SQL querying, reporting, and accounting consistency validation.
   - All 64 canonical attributes ($X_1$ through $X_{64}$) are preserved losslessly in a structured `raw_ratios JSONB` document column. This prevents schema bloat, handles varying missingness gracefully, and ensures exact reproducibility between raw files and model input vectors.

4. **Strict Provenance & Synthetic Flagging:**
   In compliance with AGENTS.md Rule 4.3 and Rule 4.4:
   - Every `financial_record` must record `source_type` (`public_dataset`), `source_ref` (`1year.arff`, etc.), and `confidence` (`verified`).
   - All relationship edges generated for GNN modeling must explicitly specify `is_synthetic = TRUE`.

5. **Handling Extreme Values & Missingness:**
   - Attributes with high missingness (e.g., $X_{37}$ at ~45% missing, $X_{21}$ at up to 31% missing) and extreme values (e.g., $X_5, X_{55}, X_{58}$ having extreme tail multipliers from zero-denominator division in distressed firms) will be handled via robust median/indicator imputation and winsorization within the shared feature engine, rather than dropping records indiscriminately.

## Consequences
- The ML training pipeline will support distinct horizon models (12M, 24M, 36M, 48M, 60M).
- Database migrations and queries remain clean, performant, and resilient.
- No leakage of future bankruptcy information across historical statements.
- System transparency and non-causal disclosures are preserved across all layers.
